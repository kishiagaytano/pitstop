The gap

Nobody is responsible for a rider's health.

Most riders are classified as independent contractors. There is no employer, so there is no occupational health programme — and no record that anything is wrong until it becomes an emergency.

No regular screening
No blood pressure checks tied to work, no baseline, no follow-up. Risk stays invisible until a rider collapses or crashes.
No exposure monitoring
Riders breathe roadside traffic air for eight to twelve hours a day. Nobody measures what they actually take in, or where.
No occupational support
No recovery, no protective equipment, no referral pathway. Missing a day of work means losing a day of income.
Riders cannot fund this themselves — margins are thin, and motorcycle purchase loans ran at roughly three times the national consumer default rate in 2024. So Pit Stop is free at the point of use, and sold to the platforms and insurers already carrying the cost of rider risk.

The software

The part we build ourselves.

The devices collect. The software is what makes ten minutes on a forecourt add up to something — a record, a risk level, and a map of where the air is worst.

Technician app
The ten-minute workflow on one tablet. Reads the devices, saves offline, syncs when signal returns — so no reading is ever lost in a dead zone.
Rider risk level
Blood pressure, exposure hours, recovery and tenure combined into one clear level — with the reason shown, never a black box.
Exposure map
Air readings tied to route and hour, building a picture of which Metro Manila corridors cost riders the most to work.

Built by the team, validated locally. The software layer is developed in-house and reviewed with a clinical partner before any rider data is collected. Health records stay with the clinical entity; partners receive anonymised summaries only.

Expected outcomes

What a year of this should produce.

More riders screened — at a place and time that costs them nothing to attend.
Risk found earlier — high blood pressure caught during a work break, not in an emergency room.
Follow-up that holds — clear thresholds, a named referral path, and a record that persists.
Trust from riders — health data stays with the clinical partner; platforms see summaries only.
Exposure evidence — the first route-level picture of what riders actually breathe.
A model that repeats — the same workflow moves to the next forecourt, and the next city.
Designed to keep running after the pilot year — not to end with it.
